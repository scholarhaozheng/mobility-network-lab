Hierarchical traffic assignment, by hand

Ten units of flow leave Washington DC (6) and Baltimore (4), cross into the New York region through 2 ports, and reach Boston. Every slider below moves that flow; every number is recomputed from the network's 4 access costs and 2 corridor laws. Groups A to D are 4 questions asked of the same 10 units. A is the problem; B, C and D are 3 ways of computing, representing and deciding it, and the point of this page is to let you feel the difference in each, not to prove it.

Read it as

Access costs are fixed: DC pays 4 to reach port A and 5 to reach port B; Baltimore pays 3 and 4. Both origins see the same 1-hour difference, so both prefer the same port, and 1 scalar z carries the whole interface.

Corridor A: 4 + 0.2·(10 − z). Corridor B: 2 + 0.6·z. Link width is flow; the label is the time on it.

One network, 4 questions: what is the answer (A), who computes it (B), at which level (C), who may commit it (D).

Group A
Native assignment
Find the flow at which no used route can improve, and the flow that minimizes total time.
You will drag the flow until the 2 route costs meet, then let 3 algorithms find the same point.
Group B
Decomposed and distributed computation
Cut the map into regions that compute on their own block and exchange only interface quantities.
You will count messages, eliminate an interior by hand, and watch 2 regions reach consensus.
Group C
Spatial hierarchy and representation
Ask which coarse quantities the fine ones determine, and which answers a coarse state cannot give.
You will merge the 2 ports, verify a commuting square, and place sensors to see the rank.
Group D
Coordination and agentic execution
Put a cap on the port, price it, verify a plan against 3 certificates, and run the loop.
You will derate the cap, watch a stale plan be refused, and read an agent's response contract.
Group A

Native traffic assignment

Data: the network, the demands 6 and 4, the cost laws. Unknown: the path flows. Problem: user equilibrium, where every used path of an OD pair has the least time; and system optimum, where the total time is least. Returns: the flows, the costs, and a certificate.

Assumes
static; fixed demand; continuous flow; separable, nondecreasing cost laws; no capacity.
Prior knowledge
shortest paths; a convex function has 1 minimum; a matrix relation has 4 roles (forward map, solve, feasible set, optimization).
A1

Move the flow until the 2 route costs meet

The potential Φ(z) = 86 − 3z + 0.4z² has its minimum where DC is indifferent between the ports; the total time C(z) = 96 − 5z + 0.8z² has its minimum 0.625 units to the left.

Read it as

At z = 3.75 the costs tie at 9.25 for DC and 8.25 for Baltimore, and the gap g(z) = 0.8z − 3 is 0. The optimum z = 3.125 costs 88.19 in total against 88.5, and the marginal charges that make travellers choose it are 1.375 on A and 1.875 on B: a difference of 0.5, exactly what it takes to move 0.625 units.

Loading adds, the split rule decides, the label prices; equilibrium is where no used path can improve.

A2

Let an algorithm find the same point

Gradient steps on Φ, 1 Newton step, or the method of successive averages; the gap G(z) = g(z)·(z − y) is the certificate each one carries.

Read it as

A solver is judged by its certificate, not by its last iterate: the gap says how far the current flow is from an answer.

Group B

Decomposed and distributed computation

Data: Group A's problem and a partition of the network into regions by ownership. State: the block form x = [xX; z; xY] with the interior states owned by the regions and the interface z carrying the coupling. Problem: compute A's answer with each region computing only on its own block and exchanging only interface quantities. Returns: the same flows, plus the counts a distributed run is judged by: messages, scalars, sweeps.

Assumes
for 1 exchange to suffice, every route crosses the cut once; for the Schur complement to be the curvature of the regional response, the interior optimum is unique and interior.
Prior knowledge
block elimination; Newton's method; preconditioned iterations and coarse spaces; convex duality (from HPC and optimization, not from traffic).
B1

Cut the map: what stays inside, what crosses

Region X owns DC, Baltimore, the 4 access links and both demands. Region Y owns the ports, the corridors and Boston. One scalar crosses.

Read it as

Region X can evaluate its own part of the objective, FX(z) = 36 + z, without asking Y anything: the access cost is 36 whichever way the flow splits, plus 1 per unit sent to port B. Region Y owns the curvature: FY(z) = 50 − 4z + 0.4z². Their sum is Φ.

The interior stays local; the interface carries the coupling.

B2

Forward or backward? Count what crosses the cut

A second destination U behind port B (B→U costs 3; A cannot reach U). Region Y's remaining cost JY(p, s) is the cost-to-go from port p to destination s. Labels compose in (min, +): ℓ(o, s) = minp { dX(o, p) + JY(p, s) }.

Read it as

JY is 2 by 2 with 1 entry ∞: it is the only thing DC and Baltimore need to know about New York and Boston. The 444 interior nodes hidden behind Chicago's 23 ports are the same idea at scale.

Forward asks a question per origin; backward publishes the remaining cost once. The answer is identical; the traffic across the cut is not.

B3

Open corridor A: the interior responds, the boundary sees only S

Inside region Y, corridor A is now 2 parallel roads, each 4 + 0.4·flow, with w on the first. Ψ(w, z) = 76 − z + 0.2[w² + (10 − z − w)²] + 0.3z². Newton solves ∇²Ψ Δ = −∇Ψ; eliminating w leaves the Schur complement S = Ψzz − Ψzw Ψww−1 Ψwz.

Read it as

The Hessian is [[0.8, 0.4], [0.4, 1.0]] everywhere, because Ψ is quadratic. S = 1.0 − 0.4·0.4/0.8 = 0.8, and 0.8 is also Φ″: the Schur complement is the curvature of the reduced problem, what the boundary sees after the interior has answered. Freezing the interior uses 1.0 instead and lands short.

The Schur complement is what the boundary sees after the interior is eliminated.

B4

Consensus on the interface: 2 copies of z and a price

min FX(zX) + FY(zY) subject to zX = zY. ADMM: X minimizes with a penalty, Y minimizes with a penalty, the scaled price u ← u + zX − zY. Newton (B3) and ADMM are 2 methods, not a sequence.

Read it as

Two regions never see each other's objective; they see a copy of the interface and a price, and the price converges to the marginal cost that region X would have told a central solver.

Group C

Spatial hierarchy and representation

Data: a fine level and a coarse level; the spatial lineage H recording which cell each object belongs to; the typed maps MN, ML, MP, MOD carrying node balances, link flows, path flows and OD pairs between levels. Problem: state which coarse quantities the fine ones determine (the commuting test), which answers the coarse state cannot give (hidden spread; rank of an observation map), and when a unit must be refined.

Assumes
H is membership only; connectivity comes from the network, never from the cells; commuting is a structural statement and never a behavioral one.
Prior knowledge
a graph and its quotient; a tree of cells (S2: each cell has 1 parent and 4 children); observability and rank.
C1

Two graphs, two kinds of map

The cell tree carries lineage: H says which cell holds a node, nothing more. The transportation graph carries flow: the maps M aggregate it, and they must respect components, because a cell can hold 2 pieces of road that never touch.

Read it as

Lineage composes across levels, H0←2 = H0←1H1←2, and never decides connectivity. The maps decide flow, and they follow the road.

C2

Merge the 2 ports into 1 and see what the coarse state cannot say

With 1 port N, the coarse model sees only the corridor pair as 1 corridor with an equal-time split, xB = 2.5 + 0.25x, and 1 access cost per origin.

Read it as

Commuting certifies the map; it does not certify the answer.

C3

The commuting square, checked by multiplication

Loading square: ML AL,P = Ac MP. Conservation square: Dc MP = MOD DOD,P. The residual is a matrix; 0 means the coarse operators are the aggregated fine ones.

Read it as

A commuting square is a structural identity: it holds for every flow or for none, and it says nothing about which flow is right.

C4

Place the sensors and read the rank

Observation y = Π fP on the 4 path flows (DC→A, DC→B, R→A, R→B). Rank says how many directions of the flow the sensors can see; the null space is what they cannot.

Read it as

One sensor adds at most 1 to the rank, and sometimes 0: corridor A's count is already the sum of DC→A and R→A.

Group D

Coordination and agentic execution

Data: agents, each owning a plan solved at a state version; a shared-resource limit (here a cap on port B); a verifier r(x) = [D f − q; fL − A f; max(0, z − CapB)]; a live version; a declared world model. Problem: from plans that are each valid and jointly violate the limit, produce a plan that is feasible, best, and committed only at the live version; expose every number through a call whose response carries class, residual, validity and version.

Assumes
costs frozen within a round; the executed state is a solver iterate and its excess over a cap is a rate, not a queue; a learning model may propose and never verifies or commits.
Prior knowledge
a Lagrange multiplier as a price; dual ascent and primal recovery; compare-and-commit, fail-closed design.
D1

Cap port B and price it

The multiplier is the cost difference at the cap, μ* = CA(CapB) − CB(CapB); with the price added to B, nobody wants to move. The split at the tie must be declared: the price does not declare it.

Read it as

A cap moves the answer; a price makes travellers accept it; a declared split makes it a plan.

D2

Verify, then commit: 3 certificates, not 1

A plan is committed only if it is feasible (r = 0), best (the duality gap is closed), and solved at the live state version. Derate the cap and watch a plan that was valid become both stale and infeasible.

Read it as

Feasibility, optimality and the live version are 3 different certificates.

D3

Run the loop: Observe, Solve, Coordinate, Verify, Commit

From the equilibrium 3.75 toward a cap of 3 by successive averages, zk+1 = zk + (CapB − zk)/(k + 1). The executed state is a solver iterate; its excess over the cap is an excess arrival rate, not a queue.

Read it as

Compare an iterate to the cap and call the difference what it is: a rate the plan has not yet removed. A queue would need a time axis this model does not have.

D4

Ask the agent: a request and its contract

Every number leaves the system through a call whose response carries its value, its class (structural identity; exact reduction under assumptions; behavioral approximation), its residual, its validity and its version.
Live version is 8 after the derating in D2. A request at an older version gets its answer marked stale.

Read it as

A learning model may propose; it never verifies and never commits. The verifier and the version are the 2 gates it cannot bypass.

Notation, 1 symbol 1 meaning. z the interface flow (port B); w an interior flow; Φ the potential, C the total time; ∇²Ψ the Hessian and S the Schur complement, never H; H the spatial lineage; M the typed maps; Π the observation map; JY the remaining cost (cost-to-go); CapB, μ, ρ, u the capacity and its prices; r the residual; excess arrival rate zk − CapB.
